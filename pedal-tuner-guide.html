Guitar Pedal Tuner Guide

Set up silent tuning for a plugged-in guitar or bass and check whether you already have what you need.

Published by Online Guitar Tuner. How our guides are maintained · Report a correction

Do you need a tuner pedal?

If you already know you need a pedal, compare the two models for display, mute routing and power.

Start with the tuner in your amp or multi-effects unit if it is easy to activate, readable onstage, and can mute the sound reaching the audience. A separate pedal is useful if you need a dedicated footswitch or a display you can read from standing height. If your guitar has no pickup, try a clip-on tuner in a noisy room. For home practice, try the free online tuner.

Connect it for a silent tuning break

  1. Turn off the connected equipment before wiring. Connect your instrument to the tuner input. Run the tuner’s mute-capable output to the next pedal or amp input. Check the manual for the right jack.
  2. Start with the tuner near the front of the chain, ahead of distortion, modulation, or a pitch shifter, so it sees the original notes.
  3. At soundcheck, confirm that you can hear the instrument with the tuner disengaged. Engage it, pluck once, and verify that the tuner reads the note while the amplified output is silent. Disengage it and confirm the sound is back.

If the tuner reads normally but the audience still hears you, check the output jack and any separate feed. For example, a DI taking its signal before the tuner can keep sending sound to the mixer. Ask the engineer to mute that feed or agree on a signal path that includes your mute.

Check fit before buying

  • Lowest note. Check the detection range against your actual tuning, especially on extended-range basses. Use the string frequency chart or the bass tuner to identify the note.
  • Reference pitch. Confirm the range includes the A4 you use. Both examples below support A4 from 436 to 445 Hz, so players who need A4 = 432 Hz should choose another model.
  • Power and controls. Check board space, jack clearance, display visibility, voltage, polarity, and available current against the exact model’s manual.
