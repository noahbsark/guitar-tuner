Guitar Fret Buzz Guide

A tuner can show the right pitch while the string still buzzes. Compare open and fretted notes to narrow down the cause.

Published by Online Guitar Tuner. How our guides are maintained · Report a correction

Run a quick listening check

If the buzz first appeared under a capo, check its fit and pressure before drawing conclusions about the guitar’s setup.

  1. Remove the capo and tune to your intended preset. Mute adjacent strings and play the affected string open with your usual attack.
  2. Play the same string at the first few frets, then higher up. Place your finger just behind each fret and press only until the note rings cleanly. Repeat with a lighter pluck.
  3. On an electric guitar, repeat through a clean amp at a comfortable level. Note whether the buzz comes through the speaker, cuts the note short, or is audible only next to the unplugged instrument.

Use the pattern to decide what to check

What you hearFirst check
One open string buzzes, then clears when frettedCheck whether the string is seated properly at the nut. A low or worn slot is one possibility. Check other signs of nut trouble before changing anything.
It buzzes at one fret or a small group of fretsRepeat with your finger just behind the fret. If the same notes still buzz or die early, record the string and fret numbers for a technician to check for uneven or worn frets.
Several fretted notes buzz at normal attackCheck whether the problem started after a string or tuning change. A technician can check string height, neck relief, and fret condition when the pattern persists.
It only happens with a capoReposition it just behind the fret. On an adjustable capo, use enough pressure for each string to ring cleanly. Check capo fit and pressure.

A faint sound heard only unplugged may be acceptable if the amplified note rings cleanly and sustains as expected. If the noise persists with the strings muted, investigate the amp, cable, or nearby hardware instead of changing string gauge.

If the buzz started after a tuning or gauge change

If the same strings buzz only after you tune lower, compare the two tunings in the string tension calculator. Then return to the previous tuning if it is appropriate for your current set and repeat the listening check. A clean result there is useful evidence that the change in tension exposed the buzz.

If you want to keep the lower tuning, compare keeping the current set, replacing one string, or changing the full set. If the buzz started after installing a new set for the same tuning, confirm the gauge and seating before buying another.

If the same frets still buzz at your normal attack, ask a repair technician to inspect the setup before filing a nut or turning the truss rod based on the sound alone.
