How Often Should You Tune a Guitar?

Check tuning every time you play, before recording or performing, and whenever the instrument’s temperature or string tension changes.

Short answer

Check your guitar at the beginning of every practice session, lesson, rehearsal, recording session, or performance. You may not need to adjust every string, but checking takes less than a minute and prevents you from practicing pitch and chords on an out-of-tune instrument.

A practical tuning schedule

SituationWhen to check
Home practiceBefore each session and after vigorous playing
Band rehearsalBefore starting, after breaks, and before important run-throughs
Live performanceBefore the set and between songs when practical
RecordingBefore every take or group of short takes
New stringsRepeatedly during the first playing session
Temperature changeAfter the guitar acclimates to the room
Alternate tuningAfter every tuning change and again after playing briefly

Why tuning changes while you play

Picking, bending, fretting, and tremolo use continually change string tension. Your hands warm the strings, while stage lights or air conditioning change the instrument’s temperature. New strings settle around the post and bridge. Small pitch movement is normal; large or repeated movement deserves investigation.

Should you tune before or after warming up?

Do both when accuracy matters. Tune before warming up so practice begins correctly. After several minutes, check again because your hands and the room may have changed the string temperature. For recording, tune immediately before the take after the instrument has acclimated.

How often should beginners check?

Beginners should check before every practice session. An out-of-tune guitar makes it harder to recognize intervals, chords, and whether a fingering problem is actually a pitch problem. Use the free microphone tuner, then play a familiar chord as a listening check.

Do guitars go out of tune while sitting?

Yes. Temperature and humidity change wood dimensions and string tension. A guitar can also settle after being moved, restrung, or placed in a case. However, a guitar does not need to remain electronically perfect while stored; simply check it before playing.

Should you loosen the strings after playing?

No, not for ordinary daily storage. Guitars are designed to remain under normal string tension. Constantly loosening and retightening increases work, creates unnecessary tuning cycles, and may shorten string life. Loosen strings only when instructed for a specific repair, shipping method, or manufacturer procedure.

When frequent retuning is normal

  • A freshly installed string set is settling.
  • You are switching repeatedly between standard and alternate tunings.
  • You use wide bends or a floating tremolo heavily.
  • The room or stage temperature is changing.
  • You are tuning a floating bridge in several balancing passes.

When frequent retuning suggests a problem

If one string drops noticeably every few minutes, inspect its winding, tuning machine, nut slot, and bridge seating. If all strings move together, consider temperature, neck movement, or a floating bridge. Work through Why Does My Guitar Keep Going Out of Tune?

If open strings remain stable but chords sound wrong farther up the neck, the issue may be intonation, not tuning frequency. Old or damaged strings can also produce unstable readings.

Does tuning more often hurt the guitar?

Normal small adjustments do not hurt a properly maintained guitar. Problems arise from tuning to the wrong octave, raising strings far above their intended pitch, forcing damaged hardware, or using a high-tension alternate tuning without understanding the setup.

A reliable one-minute routine

  1. Select the correct tuning preset.
  2. Pluck one string at a time with moderate force.
  3. Approach each target from below.
  4. Make a second pass through all six strings.
  5. Play a familiar chord and listen.
