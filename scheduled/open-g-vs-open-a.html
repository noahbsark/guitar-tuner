Open G vs Open A Tuning

Both create open major chords, but their bass voicing and tension are not identical.

Quick answer: Open G is D G D G B D; the Open A preset here is E A E A C# E. Open A raises three standard strings and carries more tension, while Open G mostly lowers pitch.

At-a-glance comparison

FeatureOpen GOpen A
NotesD G D G B DE A E A C# E
Open chordG majorA major
Tension changeMostly lowerSeveral strings higher

Chord relationships

Each tuning uses root, third, and fifth notes of its major chord. Straight barres transpose the open harmony, but the exact bass voicing differs from simply placing a capo on the other tuning.

Safety and gauges

Open G is approachable on normal strings because strings are lowered. Open A raises D to E, G to A, and B to C#, so tension increases and appropriate gauges may be needed.

Playing choice

Open G is a common starting point for blues and slide because of its comfortable tension and low fifth in the bass. Open A provides brighter resonance in A without a capo.

How to choose

  1. Begin with Open G if you are new to open tunings.
  2. Choose Open A only after confirming gauges and target octaves.
  3. Use the exact tuning sequence required by the lesson or arrangement.
