Best Guitar Tunings for Slide Guitar

Open tunings make full major chords line up under a straight slide.

Quick answer: Open G and Open D are the safest starting points. Open E is brighter but higher tension; standard tuning works for single-note slide without relearning the fretboard.

At-a-glance comparison

TuningNotesSlide advantage
Open GD G D G B DClassic major barre with comfortable tension
Open DD A D F# A DDeep major chord and clear root layout
Open EE B E G# B EBright Open D structure one step higher
StandardE A D G B EFamiliar scales and single-note lines

Why open tunings help

When the open strings form a major chord, holding the slide straight across one fret creates another major chord. This reduces complex fretting and lets the slide control pitch and vibrato.

Action and setup

A slightly higher action can prevent the slide from striking frets, but excessively high action makes ordinary fretting sharp. Use a slide weight matched to string gauge and touch.

Intonation technique

Place the slide directly above the fret wire, not behind it, and use the fingers behind the slide to mute unwanted noise. Tune using the same light attack used for slide playing.

How to choose

  1. Start with Open G for blues-oriented rhythm and slide.
  2. Choose Open D for a lower, full-register sound.
  3. Use Open D plus capo 2 instead of raising to Open E when tension is a concern.
