Guitar Tuning Checklist Before Recording

Recording exposes small pitch problems that can disappear in casual practice.

Published by Online Guitar Tuner. How our guides are maintained · Report a correction

Quick answer: Warm up the guitar, stretch and settle suitable strings, tune with a clean signal and normal attack, check fretted chords, and verify tuning before every important take.

Prepare before the session

Install new strings early enough to settle, not moments before recording unless necessary. Confirm the chosen gauge, tuning, relief, action, and intonation. Let the instrument reach room temperature.

Tune for the performance

Use the same picking strength used in the part. Heavy attacks can start sharp, especially on low strings. Tune the stable portion while checking that the attack sounds appropriate in the actual track.

Check more than open strings

Play the main chords and important notes in the register being recorded. A centered open-string tuner cannot fix poor intonation, excessive fretting pressure, or a capo that pulls notes sharp.

Monitor between takes

Bends, energetic playing, hand warmth, and changing room temperature move pitch. Recheck quickly between takes and always before doubling a part, where small differences become obvious.

Inspection checklist

  1. Acclimate and inspect the guitar.
  2. Use settled strings and confirm setup.
  3. Bypass pitch-altering effects and tune open strings.
  4. Play the actual part and inspect critical fretted notes.
  5. Retune before each keeper take and every double.

When to get professional help

Stop if a part is cracking, a string is binding under high tension, or an adjustment requires force. Nut work, major bridge changes, and truss-rod adjustments can affect playability permanently; a qualified repair technician can diagnose the instrument before material is removed or hardware is damaged.

Keep a short tuning note with the session

Record the six open-string targets, reference pitch, string set, capo fret and the part's main playing register. For a doubled rhythm part, use the same reference and capo position on both takes. A guitar set to A4 = 432 Hz and one set to A4 = 440 Hz will disagree even if each tuner shows a centered reading.

Check the notes you actually play with a full-range chromatic tuner when they exceed this site's 45–390 Hz open-string detection range. Listen to the take in context before changing saddle settings to solve a single performance problem.
