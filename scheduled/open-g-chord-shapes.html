Open G Chord Shapes

Turn the open strings into a G chord and use simple barres for movable major chords across the neck.

Tune to Open G

This guide uses D G D G B D, from the sixth string to the first. From standard, lower the sixth string to D2, the fifth to G2, and the first to D4. Use the Open G tuner, then recheck because changing three strings alters overall tension.

Shape notation: Frets run from string 6 to string 1. A number means fret that string, 0 means open, and x means mute. The open chord has D in the bass; mute the sixth string when you want G as the lowest note.

Major chords with one barre

Because the open strings form a G major chord, barring all six strings at one fret moves the entire major shape upward. The chord root on the fifth string identifies the chord: fret 2 is A, fret 4 is B, fret 5 is C, and fret 7 is D.

Control the sixth string

The open sixth string is D, the fifth of G. It gives the open chord a broad sound but places D rather than G in the bass. Use your fretting-hand thumb or a picking-hand touch to mute string 6 when a stronger root-position chord is needed.

Practice major and minor movement

  1. Strum the open G chord and then barre A major at fret 2.
  2. Move the barre to C major at fret 5 and D major at fret 7.
  3. Practice A minor at x22212.
  4. Slide the same minor shape to B minor and C minor.
  5. Check every string separately so the barre does not mute middle strings.
