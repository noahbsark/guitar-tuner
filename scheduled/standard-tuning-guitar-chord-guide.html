Standard Guitar Chord Shapes and Tuning

Tune to E A D G B E, then build a practical foundation with eight essential open chord shapes.

Tune before practicing chords

Chord practice works best when the guitar is accurately tuned to E A D G B E, from the thick sixth string to the thin first string. Even a correct fingering sounds unsettled when one string is several cents out. Use the standard guitar tuner, then play each string again after forming your first few chords.

How to read the shapes: The six characters run from the low E string to the high E string. A number is the fret to hold, 0 means play the string open, and x means mute that string.

How to make every note ring

Place each fingertip just behind its fret rather than directly on top of the metal. Curl the fingers so they do not touch neighboring strings, keep the thumb relaxed behind the neck, and use only enough pressure to stop buzzing. Test the chord one string at a time before strumming it as a whole.

Practice chord changes efficiently

  1. Choose two shapes, such as G and C.
  2. Form the first chord slowly and check every string.
  3. Release pressure without moving the hand far from the strings.
  4. Build the second shape, then return to the first.
  5. Repeat for one minute without strumming, prioritizing accurate placement.
  6. Add a slow, steady strum only after both shapes are reliable.

Common problems

A muted note usually comes from a neighboring finger touching its string. Buzzing often means the fingertip is too far behind the fret or is not pressing firmly enough. If several chords sound sharp even after tuning, lighten the fretting pressure and check the guitar's intonation.
