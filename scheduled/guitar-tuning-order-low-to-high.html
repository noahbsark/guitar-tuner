What Order Should You Tune Guitar Strings?

A consistent order makes missed strings and large tension changes easier to manage.

Quick answer: For a fixed-bridge guitar, tune from string 6 to string 1 and repeat the pass. For a floating tremolo, alternate across the strings and expect several passes.

Fixed bridges

Low-to-high—6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1—is easy to remember and matches how tuning names are written. The exact direction matters less than checking every string and repeating after total tension changes.

Floating tremolos

On a floating bridge, tightening one string pulls the bridge forward and lowers the others. Alternating between outside and inside strings, such as 6, 1, 5, 2, 4, 3, can distribute changes more evenly.

After a major tuning change

When moving several semitones or installing strings, first bring every string close rather than perfecting one. Then make progressively finer passes until the entire instrument stabilizes.

Step-by-step check

  1. Identify whether the bridge is fixed or floating.
  2. Bring all strings near their targets.
  3. Use 6-to-1 for a fixed bridge.
  4. Use alternating passes for a floating bridge.
  5. Repeat until no string moves meaningfully after the others are adjusted.

Reliable tuning habits

Work with one string at a time, use a moderate and repeatable picking attack, and let the initial pitch settle briefly. Make the final adjustment by approaching the target from below so slack is taken out of the tuning post and nut. After all six strings are close, repeat the entire pass because every tension change can slightly affect the other strings.
