Best Guitar Tunings for Heavy Metal

Select a low tuning by interval layout and instrument setup—not only by the lowest note.

Quick answer: Drop tunings favor one-finger power chords; lowered standard tunings preserve familiar intervals. The lower you go, the more string gauge and scale length matter.

Drop versus standard layout

A drop tuning lowers the sixth string one additional whole step relative to the other five, enabling a single-fret power chord. Standard-derived tunings keep the familiar fourth-based fretboard relationships.

Clarity at low pitch

Heavy strings, long scale length, controlled pick attack, and correct intonation keep low notes defined. Excessively loose strings start sharp under attack and can sound unfocused.

Use the instrument you have

Drop D and Drop C are practical on many standard guitars. Drop A and B Standard often benefit from baritone or multiscale instruments and professional setup.

How to choose

  1. Choose the interval layout that suits the riffs.
  2. Match gauge to pitch, scale, and attack.
  3. Adjust intonation after settling on a permanent gauge.
  4. Do not force oversized strings through narrow nut slots.
