Tune-o-Matic Bridge Intonation Guide

Adjust each saddle so the fretted octave matches the tuned open string.

Quick answer: If the 12th-fret note is sharp, lengthen the string; if it is flat, shorten it. Retune the open string after every saddle movement.

Prepare the guitar

Use the intended string gauge and tuning, stretch the strings, set approximate relief and action, and place the guitar in normal playing position. Intonation is the final stage of a setup, not the first.

Measure consistently

Tune the open string precisely, then compare a lightly fretted 12th-fret note. The harmonic can confirm the octave reference, but the fretted note is the measurement that reveals compensation.

Move the saddle

Sharp means the vibrating length is too short; move the saddle away from the nut. Flat means it is too long; move the saddle toward the nut. Screw orientation varies, so observe the saddle rather than assuming clockwise behavior.

Repeat the process

Moving a saddle changes open pitch. Retune and remeasure after every small adjustment, then check chords across the neck.

Inspection checklist

  1. Stabilize strings, relief, and action.
  2. Tune one open string.
  3. Measure its lightly fretted 12th-fret note.
  4. Move the saddle a small amount in the correct physical direction.
  5. Retune and repeat before proceeding to the next string.

When to get professional help

Stop if a part is cracking, a string is binding under high tension, or an adjustment requires force. Nut work, major bridge changes, and truss-rod adjustments can affect playability permanently; a qualified repair technician can diagnose the instrument before material is removed or hardware is damaged.
