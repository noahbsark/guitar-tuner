Open D vs Open E Tuning

The same major-chord structure appears one whole step apart, with very different tension.

Quick answer: Open D is D A D F# A D and lowers strings from standard. Open E is E B E G# B E and raises several strings. Open D with a capo at fret 2 gives Open E pitch with lower string tension.

At-a-glance comparison

FeatureOpen DOpen E
NotesD A D F# A DE B E G# B E
Change from standardMostly downwardSeveral strings upward
TensionLowerHigher

Shared chord structure

Both tunings form a major chord on the open strings. The same barre and slide relationships transfer because every Open E string is one whole step above its Open D counterpart.

Tension difference

Open E raises strings 5, 4, and 3 from standard and increases load. Open D lowers strings and is usually gentler on ordinary gauges. Approach Open E carefully and consider lighter strings.

Capo solution

Tune to Open D and place a capo at the second fret to sound in Open E. This preserves the interval pattern and avoids raising the open-string tension.

How to choose

  1. Use Open D for lower tension and a deep sound.
  2. Use Open E when open pitch and resonance are specifically required.
  3. Use Open D plus capo 2 for a practical Open E alternative.

Compare them without raising tension

Open D and Open E use the same interval pattern, so you can learn and test shapes in Open D first. Place a capo at the second fret to hear those shapes at Open E concert pitch without tuning three strings upward. The capo changes the available open-string length and fret locations, but it is a practical comparison when you are unsure whether the brighter key is worth the added string tension.
