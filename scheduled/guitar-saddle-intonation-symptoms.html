Signs Your Guitar Saddle or Intonation Is Wrong

A guitar can have perfectly tuned open strings while fretted notes remain sharp or flat.

Published by Online Guitar Tuner. How our guides are maintained · Report a correction

Quick answer: Compare the tuned open string, 12th-fret harmonic, and lightly fretted 12th-fret note. A consistent mismatch points toward intonation or setup rather than the tuner.

Classic symptoms

Open chords may sound acceptable while chords higher on the neck sound sour. The 12th-fret note may read consistently sharp or flat even after the open string is centered.

Rule out technique

Heavy fretting pressure bends pitch sharp, especially on tall frets or light strings. Press only enough for a clean note and use the same attack for every measurement.

Saddle direction

On most adjustable electric bridges, a sharp fretted note requires lengthening the string and a flat note requires shortening it. Exact screw direction depends on bridge orientation.

Acoustic limitations

Many acoustic saddles have limited individual adjustment. Compensation, neck geometry, nut height, and string choice interact, so large errors deserve professional evaluation.

Inspection checklist

  1. Install settled strings and tune the open note.
  2. Check the 12th-fret harmonic.
  3. Fret the 12th note lightly and compare.
  4. Repeat several times with consistent attack.
  5. Adjust only after confirming the result is repeatable.

When to get professional help

Stop if a part is cracking, a string is binding under high tension, or an adjustment requires force. Nut work, major bridge changes, and truss-rod adjustments can affect playability permanently; a qualified repair technician can diagnose the instrument before material is removed or hardware is damaged.

Compare more than one fret

A useful diagnosis checks the open string, the 12th-fret harmonic, the fretted 12th-fret note, and several chords in the playing area. One sharp fretted note can come from finger pressure or a high fret, while a consistent trend up the neck more strongly suggests intonation. Use fresh, settled strings and normal playing pressure before moving a saddle, because the adjustment changes every fretted note on that string.

Use a tuner that can measure the octave notes

This website's microphone detector is designed for open-string presets between 45 and 390 Hz. It is not suitable for a complete 12th-fret intonation check: standard-tuned B3 becomes B4 at about 493.88 Hz, and E4 becomes E5 at about 659.26 Hz. Use a full-range chromatic or strobe-style tuner for those measurements.

Keep the guitar in normal playing position and repeat the measurement with light, consistent fretting pressure. Fender's Intonation 101 explains the comparison between fretted octave notes and harmonics. An isolated sharp note is evidence to investigate, not an instruction to file the nut.
