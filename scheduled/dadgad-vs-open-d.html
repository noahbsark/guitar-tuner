DADGAD vs Open D Tuning

They share three bass strings but create different harmony on top.

Quick answer: DADGAD is D A D G A D and sounds suspended or modal. Open D is D A D F# A D and forms a complete D major chord because of its F#.

At-a-glance comparison

FeatureDADGADOpen D
NotesD A D G A DD A D F# A D
Open harmonyDsus4/modalD major
Different stringG3F#3

One-note difference

Only the third string changes between these tunings, but that note defines the harmony. G creates a suspended fourth in DADGAD; F# supplies the major third in Open D.

Playing approach

DADGAD supports drones, ambiguous major/minor color, and Celtic-style fingerpicking. Open D makes major barres and slide chord positions immediately clear.

Switching between them

Lowering the third string from G to F# changes DADGAD to Open D. Tune downward carefully, then repeat all strings as tension settles.

How to choose

  1. Choose DADGAD for modal melodies and suspended harmony.
  2. Choose Open D for major-chord slide and simple barres.
  3. Try the same riff in both and listen to the third-string effect.

A quick listening test

Tune first to DADGAD and play the six open strings slowly. Notice that A and G create a suspended, unresolved color. Then lower the third string from G to F# for Open D and listen for the major third that makes the open strings resolve as a D major chord. This one-string comparison is the fastest way to hear why DADGAD suits modal accompaniment while Open D favors direct major-chord slide and barre shapes.
