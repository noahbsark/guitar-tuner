How to Wind Guitar Strings Around Tuning Posts

Clean descending wraps secure the string and create a reliable break angle.

Quick answer: Wind toward the center of the headstock, keep each wrap below the previous one, avoid overlaps, and use only enough wraps for the string type and post.

Correct winding direction

On a conventional three-per-side headstock, strings normally leave each post toward the centerline and travel straight through the nut. Six-in-line layouts likewise follow the intended post direction. Incorrect winding creates poor alignment and awkward tuner operation.

Why descending wraps help

Each new wrap placed below the last guides the string downward on the post, increasing break angle over the nut. Crossing or overlapping coils can shift under tension and cause unpredictable pitch changes.

Secure without knots

A clean wrap pattern and adequate turns hold normal strings. Complicated knots can make removal difficult and create sharp bends. Locking tuners use their clamp and require very little winding.

Plain and wound strings

Thin plain strings generally need more wraps than thick wound strings. Too many wraps create a tall, spring-like coil; too few may slip.

Inspection checklist

  1. Thread the string through the correct post direction.
  2. Leave a controlled amount of slack.
  3. Hold tension on the free length while turning the key.
  4. Guide every wrap below the preceding wrap.
  5. Tune, stretch, retune, and trim the safe excess.

When to get professional help

Stop if a part is cracking, a string is binding under high tension, or an adjustment requires force. Nut work, major bridge changes, and truss-rod adjustments can affect playability permanently; a qualified repair technician can diagnose the instrument before material is removed or hardware is damaged.
