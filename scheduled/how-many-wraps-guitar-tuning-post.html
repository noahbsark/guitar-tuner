How Many Wraps Should a Guitar String Have?

Use enough wraps to prevent slipping without building an unstable coil.

Published by Online Guitar Tuner. How our guides are maintained · Report a correction

Quick answer: A typical target is about 2–3 wraps on wound strings and 3–5 on plain strings; many locking tuners use little or no extra wrap; follow their maker's directions. Post design can change the recommendation.

Why gauge changes the answer

Thick wound strings grip the post with fewer turns. Thin plain strings have less friction and often need additional wraps. The objective is a tidy coil, not a fixed number applied to every string.

Post design matters

Vintage split shafts, locking tuners, slotted headstocks, and classical rollers use different stringing methods. Follow the hardware manufacturer's instructions when they conflict with general guidance.

Too few wraps

A string can slip through the post hole or settle repeatedly after tuning. Slippage is more likely on thin unwound strings and smooth posts.

Too many wraps

A large or overlapping coil compresses and shifts, slows restringing, and may run out of post height. Extra wraps do not compensate for poor installation.

Inspection checklist

  1. Identify the tuning-post type.
  2. Allow more slack for thin strings and less for thick strings.
  3. Wind in clean descending layers.
  4. Reject crossed or bunched wraps.
  5. Stretch and retune before deciding the string is slipping.

When to get professional help

Stop if a part is cracking, a string is binding under high tension, or an adjustment requires force. Nut work, major bridge changes, and truss-rod adjustments can affect playability permanently; a qualified repair technician can diagnose the instrument before material is removed or hardware is damaged.

Inspect the finished coil, not just the count

A useful coil stays on the usable post surface, descends neatly and has no crossed turns. If reaching an arbitrary wrap count would stack the string above the post hole or bunch it against the headstock, stop and consult the hardware maker's method. Do not add extra turns to a locking post simply to match a conventional tuner.

For a concrete manufacturer example, compare the different post types in Fender's setup instructions. Wrap counts are starting guidance, not proof that the nut and bridge are working correctly.
