Easiest Alternate Guitar Tunings for Beginners

Begin with tunings that change only one or two strings or preserve simple chord relationships.

Quick answer: Drop D is the easiest starting point because only the sixth string changes. Half-step down preserves all shapes; Double Drop D and DADGAD are natural next steps.

At-a-glance comparison

TuningNotesStrings changed
Drop DD A D G B E6 only
Half Step DownEb Ab Db Gb Bb EbAll, same intervals
Double Drop DD A D G B D6 and 1
DADGADD A D G A D6, 2, and 1
Open GD G D G B D6, 5, and 1

Start with one change

Drop D immediately introduces a low drone and one-finger bass power chords while keeping all five upper strings familiar. Returning to standard requires raising only the sixth string.

Keep familiar shapes

Half-step down changes every pitch equally, so chord and scale shapes stay identical. The only challenge is remembering that sounding keys are one semitone lower.

Explore open-string harmony

Double Drop D, DADGAD, and Open G introduce drones and new chord voicings without requiring upward pitch changes on normal strings.

How to choose

  1. Learn the six target notes before tuning.
  2. Change one string at a time and confirm the octave.
  3. Spend several sessions learning shapes before switching again.
  4. Write the tuning at the top of any practice notes.

Use a reversible learning routine

Write the six standard notes down before changing anything. Retune one string, play a familiar chord, and identify exactly which notes changed. Spend a short session with the new sound, then use the Standard preset to return to E A D G B E. This routine builds a clear mental map and is easier on beginners than switching among several unrelated tunings in one practice session.
