Why Does My Guitar Keep Going Out of Tune?

Use this checklist to find why a guitar drifts sharp or flat and decide what you can fix safely.

Start by identifying the pattern

Tune the guitar, play for several minutes, and check every string again. A single slipping string suggests a local problem such as winding, a tuner, a nut slot, or a worn string. All strings moving in the same direction more often points to temperature, humidity, a floating bridge, or a broad change in neck tension.

New strings are still stretching

Fresh strings commonly go flat until the wraps settle. Install them with neat windings, bring them to pitch, gently stretch each string along its length, and retune. Repeat without pulling aggressively. Too few wraps can slip; a messy overlap can also move unpredictably. Follow the full new-string settling guide for safe steps.

The string may be binding at the nut

If a string jumps in pitch while turning the key or makes a small pinging sound, it may be catching in the nut slot. The slot may be dirty, too tight for the gauge, or incorrectly shaped. Do not widen it with a random tool; nut work is easy to overdo. A repair technician can diagnose and cut the slot correctly.

Temperature and humidity changed

Strings, wood, and metal respond to environmental changes. Moving from a cold car to a warm room can shift tuning quickly. Let the instrument acclimate before making major adjustments. Store it away from heaters, direct sun, and extreme humidity.

Playing technique can pull notes sharp

A hard pick attack briefly raises pitch, especially on loose low strings. Pressing fretted notes harder than necessary also stretches them sharp. Tune with the same moderate attack you normally use and compare open-string tuning with gently fretted notes.

Check the hardware

  • Confirm tuner mounting screws and string-post nuts are secure, but do not overtighten.
  • Look for a string slipping at the bridge or tuning post.
  • On a floating tremolo, changing one string changes the balance of the others; tune in several passes.
  • Check that the string is seated correctly at the bridge and nut.

If one tuning key feels loose, stiff, or uneven, use the tuning peg troubleshooting guide before replacing hardware.

Tuning versus intonation

If open strings tune correctly but chords sound increasingly wrong higher up the neck, the issue may be intonation rather than tuning stability. Old strings can also intonate inconsistently, so replace a worn set before adjusting bridge saddles.

A practical troubleshooting order

  1. Use the online tuner in a quiet room.
  2. Check string age, installation, and winding.
  3. Let the guitar reach room temperature.
  4. Listen for nut binding and inspect hardware.
  5. Compare the open note with the 12th-fret harmonic and fretted note.
  6. If the problem persists or the instrument needs structural adjustment, consult a qualified technician.
