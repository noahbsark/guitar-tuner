Why Guitar Strings Break

The spot where the string broke tells you what to check before you put on a replacement.

Published by Online Guitar Tuner. How our guides are maintained · Report a correction

What to do first

If the string just snapped, set the guitar down and keep your face clear of the loose ends. Once the string is slack, note where it broke before removing it. If it came loose intact at the post or bridge, check the winding and attachment.

Check where it broke

Break locationWhat to check
Bridge or saddleIf a second string breaks where it crosses the saddle, check for a groove or rough edge. On pin-bridge acoustics, check that the ball end was seated correctly. On a tie-block classical, first check whether the tail simply pulled through.
Nut or tuning postLook for a sharp edge where the string crossed the nut or entered the post. If you heard a ping or saw the pitch jump as you tuned, check for a binding nut slot.
Between the nut and bridgeLook for corrosion or a kink. If a new string breaks while tuning, confirm the peg, note, and octave before trying again. In standard tuning the high E is E4 and the low E is E2.

If the same string keeps breaking at the same contact point, have a guitar technician inspect it before using another replacement. Filing a nut or saddle changes how the string sits.

Replace one string or the whole set?

For a single replacement, find the gauge on your old set’s packaging or manufacturer’s chart. Match both the gauge and whether the string is plain or wound.

Replace just the broken string if the rest of the set is fresh and you can match its gauge and construction. For example, match a plain .010 with a plain .010. If the other strings are rusty, dull, or worn, or you cannot identify the set and want an even response, consider a matched set. Use nylon on a guitar designed for nylon and check the bridge fastening on a classical guitar.

Install the replacement and tune it

Follow the instructions for your bridge and tuning posts. Bring the replacement up slowly and verify the note and octave. On a floating bridge, recheck the rest of the strings after the replacement reaches pitch.
