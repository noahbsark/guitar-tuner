What is Open G tuning?

Open G uses D G D G B D, low to high. Strumming the open strings produces the notes of a G major chord: G, B, and D. From standard tuning, the sixth, fifth, and first strings are lowered; the fourth, third, and second strings remain unchanged.

How to tune from standard to Open G

  1. Lower the sixth string from E2 to D2.
  2. Lower the fifth string from A2 to G2.
  3. Leave the fourth string at D3, third at G3, and second at B3.
  4. Lower the first string from E4 to D4.
  5. Strum softly and recheck each string after the tension settles.

Playing in Open G

A straight barre across strings 5 through 1 creates a movable major chord. The open position is G major, the fifth fret is C major, and the seventh fret is D major. This direct relationship makes the tuning useful for slide guitar, blues, roots music, and riff-based accompaniment.

The lowest string is D rather than G, so it can add a low fifth beneath the chord. Some arrangements mute or avoid that string when a cleaner G bass is wanted.

Open G tips

  • Use light slide pressure directly above the fret to avoid sharp notes.
  • Standard chord diagrams will not sound the same; use Open G shapes.
  • Most standard string sets handle the three lowered strings comfortably.
  • Check the first string carefully because it may drift after being lowered to D.
