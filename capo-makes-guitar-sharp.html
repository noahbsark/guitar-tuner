Compare three readings on one string

  1. Remove the capo and tune the open string. Pick a string that sounds off with the capo on.
  2. Play that string at the capo fret with your finger, pressing only enough to get a clean note. Let the initial attack settle, then note the pitch and cents.
  3. Install the capo just behind that fret, on the nut side of the fret wire. Keep the pad straight across the strings. Pluck the same string without fretting it by hand and compare the two readings.

For example, at fret 2 the open G3 string should sound A3. If it reads +2 cents by hand and +11 cents with the capo, the change associated with clamping is about 9 cents. Repeat the comparison before acting on a single reading.

What to change based on the result

What you hear or measureWhat to try
Sharper only with the capoRe-seat it close behind the fret and reduce adjustable pressure until all strings still ring. Check that the pad closes without dragging the strings sideways.
One edge buzzes while the other edge goes sharpCheck the fit across the fretboard at that fret. A mismatched curve or pad width can produce uneven pressure.
The note is sharp both by hand and with the capoTry a lighter fretting touch. If the error repeats without the capo, check the 12th-fret octave and investigate the setup separately.
The meter shows a different note or octaveCheck the fret and string, mute the other strings, and pluck again. Compare the displayed note with the expected target before adjusting.

Once the strings ring cleanly, recheck all six with the capo on. If you make a small tuning adjustment for the song, check open tuning again when you remove the capo.

When a different capo may help

Keep your current capo if repositioning or adjusting it fixes the readings. If a fixed-pressure capo repeatedly pulls notes sharp after you’ve tested placement and fit, compare adjustable capos by neck width and fretboard curve. Shubb explains how its pressure adjustment and fretboard fit work.
