What is Open E tuning?

Open E uses E B E G# B E. The open strings spell an E major chord. The sixth, second, and first strings remain at their standard notes, while the fifth, fourth, and third strings are tuned upward. That higher tension makes Open E different from most alternate tunings.

Tension warning: Tune upward slowly. Increased tension can be hard on old strings, lightly built instruments, and some acoustic guitars. If you are unsure, use Open D with a capo at the second fret to get the same sounding pitches with lower string tension.

How to tune from standard to Open E

  1. Leave the sixth string at E2.
  2. Raise the fifth string from A2 to B2.
  3. Raise the fourth string from D3 to E3.
  4. Raise the third string from G3 to G#3.
  5. Leave the second and first strings at B3 and E4.
  6. Pause, then recheck every string without overtightening past the target.

Playing in Open E

The open position is E major. A barre at the fifth fret produces A major, and a barre at the seventh fret produces B major. The tuning supports slide, blues, and arrangements that use ringing E-major harmony. Standard chord shapes do not transfer directly.

Safer alternatives and setup

  • Open D plus a capo at fret 2 produces the same sounding open chord as Open E.
  • Use fresh strings in good condition and stop if a string feels or sounds distressed.
  • Do not leave a delicate instrument at elevated tension if its maker or repair technician advises against it.
  • When returning to standard, lower strings 5, 4, and 3 carefully.
