Drop D Chord Shapes

Use Drop D's low D string for powerful one-finger chords, wide open voicings, and familiar upper-string shapes.

Published by Online Guitar Tuner. How our guides are maintained · Report a correction

Start in Drop D

Drop D is D A D G B E. Begin in standard tuning and lower only the sixth string from E2 to D2 with the Drop D tuner. The upper five strings remain unchanged, so many familiar shapes still work.

Shape notation: Frets are shown from the sixth string to the first. A number is the fret, 0 is open, and x is muted.

Why the one-finger power chord works

The sixth and fourth strings are both D notes one octave apart, with A on the fifth string between them. Fretting all three at the same fret preserves that root–fifth–octave relationship. Moving the barre therefore produces a complete power chord at every fret.

Keep the low string controlled

The dropped sixth string is looser than in standard tuning and can sound sharp if struck too hard. Use a controlled pick attack, mute unused bass strings with the picking hand, and tune using the same attack strength you normally play with.

Five-minute practice routine

  1. Play D5, E5, F5, G5, and A5 slowly.
  2. Check that only strings 6–4 ring for each power chord.
  3. Alternate D5 and the full D major shape.
  4. Practice changing between G major and D major.
  5. Finish by retuning the low D string after it has been played firmly.
